My research connects the study of microbial communities in their natural environments with methods that help explain how they function. I combine molecular microbiology, genome-resolved metagenomics, and metabolomics to investigate the relationships between microorganisms, their hosts, and their surroundings.

## Microbial communities in Andean páramo wetlands

My doctoral research focuses on Chingaza, a high-elevation wetland in the Colombian Andes. Páramos store substantial amounts of soil carbon and help regulate water supplies, but the microbial communities underlying these processes remain poorly understood.

I investigate uncultivated microbial lineages and their potential roles in soil carbon cycling. I developed a workflow that connects raw metagenomic sequences to genome recovery, abundance estimates, taxonomic classification, and potential functional annotation.

I am also leading a multi-omics project integrating metagenomics, untargeted GC-MS/MS and LC-MS/MS metabolomics, gas-flux measurements, and soil biogeochemistry. By examining these datasets together, I aim to understand how variation in microbial communities and available metabolites relates to carbon cycling and greenhouse-gas production.

My involvement spans field sampling in Colombia, DNA and metabolite extraction, computational analysis, and interpretation. Related work examines how endemic plant species and soil–root compartments structure microbial communities.

## Microbial engineering and dietary mercury

Previously, I worked on developing a probiotic strategy to reduce dietary mercury bioavailability in the gut.

I designed constructs, cloning strategies, and screening assays, and programmed Opentrons liquid-handling robots to screen bacterial strains across a range of mercury concentrations. I also contributed to mouse-study design, toxicity and dosing experiments, sample collection, and data analysis. Preliminary findings helped guide the selection of strains, microbial communities, and mercury concentrations for subsequent experiments.

## Questions I want to pursue next

I am interested in understanding microbial communities as interacting, evolving systems. How do metabolite exchange, competition, and spatial organization influence what a community can do? When do interactions between species remain stable, and how does genetic exchange change those relationships?

In my postdoctoral work, I hope to combine experiments (i.e. synthetic communities, microbial engineering, HGT), and computational analysis to test these questions. My goal is to understand how interactions among microorganisms produce functions that cannot be explained by studying each organism alone.
